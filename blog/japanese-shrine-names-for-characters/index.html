Real Japanese Shrines and Temples You Can Name a Character After

48 real Japanese shrines (神社) and temples (寺), grouped by region. Each entry has its romaji, kanji, type, location, and the “significance” field copied from the anime site’s public word bank. The “how to use” section shows how to turn the place part of any entry into a character surname, clan name, or story setting.

A note before you read on. Every place on this page is a real, active sacred site. Using a place name for a character is normal in fiction — Japanese writers do it themselves, and many real Japanese surnames come from place names. Treat the place itself with respect: no mocking tone, no sexualised or demeaning use, no “the cursed shrine” gag. The kanji and significance field are here so you can use the name accurately, not as a prop.

How to use these names

These are real, active sacred places, not character names. Take the place part of each entry and use it as a Japanese-style surname, clan name, or story setting. Drop the category word (shrine / jinja / jingu / temple / -ji) and use what’s left. So Shiogama Shrine becomes Shiogama, and Kinkakuji becomes Kinkaku. Pair with a given name from the IRL Names bank. Note: dropping the suffix is a naming choice, not an official name.

Worked examples from data/given-names.json:

  • Shiogama Yui — Shiogama from Shiogama Shrine (place = Shiogama) + Yui 結衣 (female given name, tie + garment). Reads as a Miyagi-born OC with a coastal-aura surname.
  • Hakone Haruto — Hakone from Hakone Shrine (place = Hakone) + Haruto 陽翔 (male given name, sun + soar). A mountain-protector-coded male character.
  • Kamo Ren — Kamo from Kamo Shrine (place = Kamo) + Ren 蓮 (male given name, lotus). A Sendai-region OC with a calm, classical surname.
  • Kanda Sakura — Kanda from Kanda Shrine (place = Kanda) + Sakura 桜 (female given name, cherry blossom). A Tokyo-coded female surname with floral given name.
  • Atago Sora — Atago from Atago Shrine (place = Atago) + Sora 空 (neutral given name, sky). A gender-neutral pairing that reads as a fire-and-sky themed name.
  • Kashii Mei — Kashii from Kashii Shrine (place = Kashii) + Mei 芽依 (female given name, sprout + rely). A Fukuoka-region female OC.
  • Katori Akira — Katori from Katori Shrine (place = Katori) + Akira 明 (neutral given name, bright, given-names index neutral/0). A Chiba-coded, gender-neutral character with a warrior-aura surname.
  • Kinkasan Riku — Kinkasan from Kinkasan Shrine (place = Kinkasan) + Riku 陸 (male given name, land). An island-aura surname with a grounded given name.

Dropping the suffix is the most common move but isn’t required. From Fushimi Inari, Fushimi works well on its own — it is also a real Japanese surname. Kinkakuji drops to Kinkaku (“Golden Pavilion”), and Ginkakuji to Ginkaku. Use whatever fits.

For a setting rather than a name, the kanji and the location field give you what the place looks like. Chokaisan Omonoimi on Mount Chokai, or the three Dewa Sanzan shrines (Gassan, Haguro, Yudonosan) on the three sacred peaks of Yamagata, are natural pilgrimage-style backdrops.

Glossary (terms that appear in the data)

  • Ichinomiya — the shrine ranked highest in a province or region. Several data entries carry this label (Hokkaido, Tsugaru, Shiogama, Futarasan, Katori).
  • Jingu — a title (suffix) used for certain high-ranking shrines, many with imperial ties. Hokkaido Jingu, Ise Jingu, Katori Jingu, Atsuta Jingu.
  • Taisha — a high-rank shrine suffix. Fushimi Inari Taisha and Sumiyoshi Taisha carry it in this list.
  • Toshogu — a shrine built for or related to Tokugawa Ieyasu. Data: Hirosaki Toshogu, Nikko Toshogu, Kunozan Toshogu.
  • Hachiman — the deity of war and protector of the nation. Usa Shrine is the head shrine of all Hachiman.
  • Inari — the deity of rice, agriculture, and business. Fushimi Inari and Taikodani Shrine (an Inari shrine) appear in the data.
  • Gokoku — “protector-of-the-nation” shrines. Akita Gokoku Shrine is the Akita-prefecture one.
  • Tenmangu — a shrine for the deity of learning, originally Sugawara no Michizane. Dazaifu Tenmangu is the head shrine.
  • Sumiyoshi Sanjin — the three Sumiyoshi deities of poetry and seafarers, worshipped at Sumiyoshi Shrine in Osaka.
  • Dewa Sanzan — the three sacred mountains of Yamagata. Data lists Gassan, Haguro, Yudonosan as the three shrines on the three peaks.
  • Sannō — “mountain king.” Hie Shrine is the main Sannō shrine in Tokyo.
  • Kusanagi — the sacred sword, one of the three Imperial Regalia, housed at Atsuta Shrine.
