How to Pick an Anime Name for Your OC

A practical 6-step method for naming your anime OC. Works whether you're writing fanfic, building a VTuber persona, naming a Discord handle, or just want a name that fits your character. No fluff — just a simple process you can follow yourself.

Most OC name lists give you a thousand random names and call it a day. That's not what you want. You want your character to have a name that fits them. The way to get there is a short, repeatable method — pick the character first, then let the name follow.

Step 1

Decide the OC's gender (or skip it)

Most OCs have a gender. Some don't. Japanese given names are gender-marked by tradition — haruto (陽翔) is read as masculine, himari (陽葵) is feminine. If gender doesn't matter for your character, use the gender-neutral set instead: akira (明, bright), sora (空, sky), nagi (凪, calm).

Try it: open the IRL Names tab → Male, Female, or Gender-neutral.
Step 2

Decide the OC's era

Is your character modern-day, historical, fantasy, or sci-fi? The era changes what kind of name fits. A samurai-era OC wants yamato (大和) or asahi (朝陽) — a "great harmony" or "morning sun" name. A modern OC can take ren (蓮, lotus) or yuma (悠真) without anyone blinking. Sci-fi or fantasy OCs often want something that sounds Japanese without being a real person's name — that's where the Mythology tab comes in.

Era → generator mapping:
• Modern → IRL Names (male/female/neutral)
• Historical / feudal → IRL Names (male) or Mythology → figures
• Fantasy / mythological → Mythology → figures (yokai, deities, shrines)
• Sci-fi / cyberpunk → Mythology → martial (kengo, kensei, daimyo)
Step 3

Pick a vibe, not just a meaning

Two characters can both be "strong" but in different ways. One is dignified (rin 凛, "dignified, cold-crisp"), the other is epic (amaterasu 天照, the sun goddess). Don't pick a name by meaning alone — pick by feel. The anime site's data is hand-curated with vibe tags for this reason.

Six common vibes for anime OCs:

  • Cute — himari 陽葵, koharu 心春, usagi 兎 (rabbit)
  • Cool — kengo 剣豪, kitsune 狐 (fox)
  • Dark / mysterious — tsukuyomi 月読 (moon god), komainu 狛犬
  • Strong / epic — yamato 大和, orochi 大蛇 (eight-headed serpent)
  • Elegant — suzu 鈴 (bell), akari 明里
  • Vocab-as-name — midori 緑 (green), sora 空 (sky)
Try it: in the IRL Names or Mythology sections, click different vibe buttons (cute, cool, dark, strong, elegant) to filter by feel.
Step 4

Choose a format

Now decide how the name should be shaped:

  • Standalone — just a Japanese given name (or a mythological name). Clean. Best for: any era, any genre. The default for OCs.
  • Paired — a mythology name + a real given name (e.g., Inari Sakura, Fujin Hinata). Best for: shrine maiden OCs, deity OCs, names that hint at a story.
  • Fusion — pair your own word with a Japanese word. ShadowKitsune, StarYuki, WolfTsukuyomi. CamelCase, spaced, hyphenated, underscored, lowercase, or piped — your pick. Best for: handles, usernames, fan-persona names.
  • Cute — Japanese animal/nature words with cute suffixes (-chan, -maru, -pon). E.g., UsagiMaru, KitsuneChan. Best for: pet names, mascot handles, kawaii OCs.
Try it: open the Fusion tab and type any word — your character name, your favorite animal, a single syllable — and pair it with a Japanese word. Use the ⇄ button to flip the order without regenerating.
Step 5

Test the name out loud

Before you commit, say the name out loud five times. Count how it sounds at:

  • Fast — when your friend says "follow my OC @ ___" quickly. Does it survive?
  • Across a noisy room — would someone hear it on Discord or in a TikTok comment?
  • In anger / sadness — would the name still work if the character is yelling or crying?
  • To a 5-year-old — can they say it without stumbling? (Tsukuyomi can trip up non-Japanese speakers; short names like Sora or Rin pass easily.)

If it fails any of these, regenerate. Don't get attached until it survives the read-aloud test.

Step 6

Save 3–5 finalists, then pick one

Don't pick from a single generated batch. Save 3–5 names you like (the generator has a ⭐ Save button on every result), then go away for an hour. Come back, re-read them. The one that still feels right is your answer.

Why this works: the first name you generate is usually the most obvious one — the words your brain was already reaching for. The 4th or 5th is often the one that's actually right but doesn't reveal itself until you've seen the others.

FAQ

Is it OK to use a real Japanese person's name for a character?

Yes, with one rule of thumb: don't use a famous real person's name (a living celebrity, a recent political figure, a known historical person). For everyday real names (which is what the IRL Names tab pulls from — these are entries in Japanese baby-name registries), it's fine. Common names like Haruto and Himari are shared by many real people, so using one for a character doesn't point to any particular person. The names carry meaning, not identity.

What if my OC is genderfluid / non-binary?

Use the gender-neutral filter. Real Japanese names like Sora, Hikari, Akira, Nagi work for any gender. The Mythology tab is also good for this — Suzaku, Fujin, Tsukuyomi don't have an inherent gender.

My OC has multiple names (first + last). What now?

Pick the given name with the IRL Names tab, then pair it with a real surname from the Surnames data (the generator's IRL Names section does this automatically). The most common Japanese surnames are Sato, Suzuki, Takahashi, Tanaka, Watanabe — all real, all usable.

Should I use kanji or hiragana?

Either. Kanji (陽翔) looks more "real name" and reads more visually striking. Hiragana (はると) reads softer and is common for nicknames / younger characters. The name works either way — kanji and hiragana are different writings of the same name.
